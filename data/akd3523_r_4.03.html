5

The effect of mixture strength on exhaust smoke.
  1. TOO WEAK: Irregular note, splashy misfire, and colourless.
  2. CORRECT: Regular and even note.
  3. TOO RICH: Regular or rhythmical misfire, blackish.

6

  1. Check for correct mixture by gently pushing the lifting pin (4) up about 1/32 in. (1 mm.) after free movement has been taken up.
  2. The graph illustrates the effect on engine r.p.m. and indicated mixture strength when the piston is raised.
    RICH MIXTURE: r.p.m. increase considerably.
    CORRECT MIXTURE: r.p.m. increase very slightly.
    WEAK MIXTURE: r.p.m. immediately decrease.
  3. Re-adjust the mixture strength if necessary.

7

  1. Reconnect the mixture control wire with about 1/16 in. (2 mm.) free movement before it starts to pull on the jet lever.
  2. Pull the mixture control knob until the linkage is about to move the carburetter jet operating arm and adjust the fast-idle screw (2) to give an engine speed of about 1,000 r.p.m. when hot.
  3. Return the control knob and check that there is some clearance between the fast-idle screw (2) and the throttle stop.

8

Finally top up the piston damper with the recommended engine oil until the level is 1/2 in. (13 mm.) below the top of the hollow piston rod.

Note
On non-dustproofed carburetters, identified by a vent hole in the piston damper top, the oil level should be 1/2 in. (13 mm.) above the top of the hollow piston rod.
